    India, officially the Republic of India, is the world's largest democracy and one of its most populous countries. It sits in South Asia, bordered by Pakistan to the west; China, Nepal and Bhutan to the north; Bangladesh and Myanmar to the east; and the Indian Ocean to the south. Its recorded history spans over 5,000 years, taking in ancient civilisations, and lasting contributions to science, philosophy and art.

    More than 1.4 billion people speak over 1,600 languages and practise Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism and Jainism, among others — a plurality that shapes nearly every subject below.

    Geography

    India's geography runs from the Himalaya in the north to tropical coast in the south. It is conventionally divided into six major regions.

    The Himalaya

    The Himalaya, which include Mount Everest at 8,848 metres, form the northern boundary. The range supports a wide variety of wildlife and is the source of much of the country's water supply.

    Northern plains

    Formed by the convergence of the Ganges, Indus and Brahmaputra, these plains are fertile and support a substantial share of India's population and agriculture.

    Peninsular plateau

    Characterised by ancient rock formations, this region includes the Deccan Plateau, known for rich mineral deposits and diverse flora and fauna.

    Thar Desert

    Also known as the Great Indian Desert, the Thar is the largest desert in South Asia, spanning Rajasthan and Gujarat.

    Coastal plains

    The Malabar, Konkan and Coromandel coasts are known for their beaches and for marine biodiversity.

    Islands

    The Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep archipelagos offer landscapes and cultures distinct from the mainland.

    History

    Six periods account for most of the political shape of the subcontinent.

    Indus Valley Civilisation (3300–1300 BCE)

    One of the world's earliest civilisations, known for well-planned cities such as Mohenjo-daro and Harappa.

    Mauryan Empire (322–185 BCE)

    Founded by Chandragupta Maurya, one of the largest empires in ancient India, with Ashoka as its most famous emperor.

    Gupta Empire (320–550 CE)

    Often called India's golden age, a period of significant advancement in science, mathematics and literature.

    Mughal Empire (1526–1857)

    Established by Babur, ruling much of the subcontinent and leaving architecture including the Taj Mahal.

    British rule (1757–1947)

    The British East India Company, and later the Crown, governed India, producing far-reaching economic and political change.

    Independence (1947)

    India gained independence on 15 August 1947 and became a republic in 1950.

    Culture

    Languages

    India recognises 1,652 languages. Hindi and English are official; Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi and Urdu are among the most widely spoken of the rest.

    Religions

    India is constitutionally secular. Hinduism is the majority religion, followed by Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism and Jainism.

    Festivals

    Diwali, the festival of lights; Holi, the festival of colours; and Eid al-Fitr, marking the end of Ramadan, are observed nationally. Regional festivals such as Pongal, Bihu and Durga Puja are celebrated with comparable scale.

    Traditional arts

    Classical dance forms including Bharatanatyam and Kathak, and the Carnatic and Hindustani musical traditions, sit alongside handicrafts in pottery, textiles and jewellery.

    Economy

    India's economy is the world's fifth-largest by nominal GDP and third-largest by purchasing power parity. It is a mixed economy of private and public enterprise, with information technology, pharmaceuticals, agriculture, textiles and manufacturing as its principal sectors.
